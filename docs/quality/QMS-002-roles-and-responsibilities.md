## 1. Purpose

To assign and communicate the responsibilities and authorities for the quality management system, so that it is clear who decides what — including where a contractor may decide and where they may not.

Satisfies ISO 9001:2015 clause 5.3, and records the leadership commitments of 5.1.

## 2. Why this is needed in a one-person business

It looks unnecessary when one person holds every role. It is not, for two reasons.

**The first is contractors.** The moment someone else touches client work, the boundary between what they may decide and what they must refer becomes the thing that protects the client. Writing it down before the first contractor is engaged is far easier than after a dispute.

**The second is continuity.** If the owner is unavailable, the list below is what tells someone else what has to happen and who must be told.

## 4. Responsibilities and authorities

### 4.1 Top management — clause 5.1

Accountable for:

- The quality management system achieving its intended results
- The quality policy and objectives being set and compatible with the direction of the business
- The QMS requirements being integrated into how work is actually done, rather than run alongside it
- Resources being available for the QMS, including the budget for an external internal auditor
- Communicating why the QMS matters, and why conforming to it matters
- Promoting improvement and risk-based thinking
- Holding the quarterly management review

### 4.2 Decision authority

| Decision | Authority | May be delegated to a contractor? |
|---|---|---|
| Approve a quality or security policy | Owner | **No** |
| Approve or change an SOP | Owner | **No** |
| Accept a client's requirements as complete, and issue a quote | Owner | **No** |
| Agree a change to scope, price or delivery date with a client | Owner | **No** |
| Authorise release of work to a client | Owner | **No** |
| Accept a nonconformity rather than correct it | Owner, with the client's written agreement | **No** |
| Close a corrective action as verified | Owner | **No** |
| Grant access to client systems or data | Owner | **No** |
| Design decisions within an agreed requirement | Owner or contractor | **Yes**, within the written brief |
| Technical implementation choices with no effect on scope, cost or date | Owner or contractor | **Yes** |
| Raising a nonconformity or a corrective action | Anyone doing work, including contractors | **Yes** — and encouraged |

**The pattern:** anything affecting a client's cost, date, data or what they receive is the owner's alone. Anything about how the work is done inside an agreed requirement can be delegated.

### 4.3 Contractor responsibilities

A contractor engaged under SOP-SUP-001 §7 is responsible for:

- Working to the written brief and its acceptance criteria
- Raising anything ambiguous in the brief before building on the assumption
- Raising a nonconformity when they find one, in their own work or anyone else's
- Complying with POL-002 and their confidentiality undertaking
- Using only the access granted, and telling the owner when it is no longer needed

A contractor is **not** authorised to communicate with a client about scope, price or dates, or to release work to a client, unless that is stated in writing in their brief.
